SSD vs HDD: Which One Should You Buy?

A simple comparison for laptops, desktops and external storage.

Speed

SSDs have no spinning disks and generally deliver much faster startup, file access and application loading than traditional HDDs.

Capacity and price

HDDs can offer large capacities at a lower cost per gigabyte, which can make them useful for bulk storage and backups.

Durability

SSDs have no moving mechanical parts, which can be an advantage in laptops and portable drives. However, all storage devices can fail, so backups remain essential.

Noise and power

HDDs contain moving parts and can make audible sounds while operating. SSDs are silent and can be efficient in many laptop workloads.

Which should you choose?

For an operating-system drive or a laptop, an SSD is usually the better everyday experience. For large archives and secondary backups where speed is less important, an HDD can still make sense.
